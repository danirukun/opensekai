## R — Requirements

### Calibration (TRK-04, TRK-05, TRK-07)

R1. A tracking source entity gets an optional field `calibration: { bones: Record<CalibrationBone, { rotation: Quat; space: 'raw' | 'normalized' }> }`. `CalibrationBone` is one of `hips`, `spine`, `chest`, `upperChest`, `neck`, `head`. The record holds 1 to 6 entries. Each rotation is a unit quaternion (the existing `quat` rule). A missing field means no calibration. TypeScript (`packages/contracts`) and Rust (`crates/contracts`) accept and write the same shape: in Rust the field is `Option` with `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "Option::is_none")]`, as `smoothing` on the Tracking entity, so a missing field is not written back as `null`. An old show without the field loads as today.
R2. A new patch operation `setTrackingCalibration { entityId, calibration }` sets the field, or removes it when `calibration` is `null`. It fails with `INVALID_INPUT` when the entity is not a tracking source. It does not change the source `generation`.
R3. A new patch operation `calibrateTracking { entityId }` captures a calibration. The session resolves it before `applyOperations`, against the scene before the patch: it reads the current smoothed sample of the source from the tracking composer (the value that `evaluate` uses, before the stale fade), takes each `CalibrationBone` that the sample has, with its rotation and space, and replaces the operation with `setTrackingCalibration`. The session clock for this check is the `trackingNowMs ?? elapsedMs` of the last `advance`; the session stores it in each `advance`. The operation fails with `INVALID_INPUT` and the source ID when no `advance` ran yet, when the source is disabled, when it has no sample of its current generation, when the sample is older than 500 ms at that clock, or when the sample has no `CalibrationBone`. A patch that has `calibrateTracking` and also `setTrackingConfig` or `removeEntity` for the same source fails with `INVALID_INPUT`, because the capture uses the scene before the patch. Other operations in the same patch apply in order, as for each patch. Rust validates the shape only.
R4. In `evaluate`, the composer applies the calibration of a source to its sample before mapping (R10) and before mixing. For each calibrated bone that the sample has with the same space, `rotation' = inverse(neutral) × rotation`, normalized. A bone with a different space, or a bone that the calibration does not name, does not change. Thus the captured pose becomes identity, which is the sender's zero for a raw bone and the rest pose for a normalized bone. One exception keeps a MediaPipe face correct when the chest moves: when the sample and the calibration both have a normalized `chest` and a normalized `head`, and the sample has no `neck` and no `upperChest`, the head is calibrated in the frame above the chest: `head' = inverse(chest') × inverse(chestN × headN) × (chest × head)`, where `chestN` and `headN` are the saved neutrals and `chest'` is the calibrated chest. Thus `chest' × head' = inverse(chestN × headN) × (chest × head)`, which is the calibrated face in world space that the B5 head and chest composition then restores. The stale fade, hold, and that composition apply after calibration, as today.
R5. `setTrackingConfig` keeps the calibration, with one exception: for a MediaPipe source, a change of `mirror` removes the calibration, because the mirror changes the sign of the captured rotations. `sceneReplace` and show load keep the saved calibration. Calibrate replaces a calibration, and Reset removes it. The editor has no scene undo (OSE-09), so this slice adds none.
R6. The MediaPipe retargeter no longer keeps a neutral. The head rotation that it sends is the face rotation in camera space (identity when the face looks along the camera axis), composed with the chest as today. `createRetargeter().calibrate`, the worker `calibrate` request, and the tracker `calibrate()` are removed.
R7. The tracking source Inspector of an iFacialMocap or MediaPipe source shows a **Calibrate** button next to Start/Stop. It is enabled when the source state is `receiving`. It sends one `scenePatch` with `calibrateTracking`. When the source has a calibration, a **Reset** button next to it sends `setTrackingCalibration` with `null`. A failed patch shows its error message in the existing alert place. VMC sources do not show the buttons, because the VMC sender calibrates; MCP can still calibrate a VMC source. The callbacks go through the same path as smoothing: `apps/desktop/src/editor/model.ts`, `EditorApp.svelte`, and `Inspector.svelte`.

### Face mapping (TRK-04, TRK-05)

R8. A tracking source entity gets an optional field `mapping`:
`{ headIntensity: number; headOffset: Vec3; eyeIntensity: number; blendShapeSensitivity: number; linkedBlink: boolean; blendShapes: Record<ArkitName, { inputMin: number; inputMax: number; outputMin: number; outputMax: number }> }`.
Limits: `headIntensity` and `eyeIntensity` 0 to 2; `headOffset` each axis −90 to 90 (degrees; the quaternion is `qY(y) × qX(x) × qZ(z)`, Euler order YXZ as B5 R18, right-handed, so a positive `y` turns +Z toward +X); `blendShapeSensitivity` 0 to 3; for each range, `0 ≤ inputMin < inputMax ≤ 1` and `0 ≤ outputMin ≤ outputMax ≤ 1`; `blendShapes` keys are names from `ARKIT_NAMES`, at most 52. The default (`DEFAULT_TRACKING_MAPPING`) is intensity 1, offset `[0, 0, 0]`, sensitivity 1, linked blink off, no ranges. A missing field means the default. TypeScript and Rust accept and write the same shape.
R9. A new patch operation `setTrackingMapping { entityId, mapping }` sets the field. It fails with `INVALID_INPUT` when the entity is not a tracking source. It does not change the source `generation`.
R10. After calibration (R4), the composer applies the mapping of a source to its sample, in this order:
 1. Head: when the sample has `head`, scale its rotation angle by `headIntensity` about the same axis (start from the shortest arc, angle 0 to 180°; 0 gives identity; a scaled angle above 180° is kept as a rotation about the same axis, so it reads as the shorter turn the other way), then `rotation' = offset × rotation`, where `offset` is the quaternion of `headOffset`. For a normalized head the offset turns about the parent axes of the pose frame. For a raw head (iFacialMocap, VMC) it turns about the parent axes of the sender's head node; the evidence file records that the raw case is not checked against a real sender.
 2. Eyes: scale the rotation angle of `leftEye` and `rightEye` by `eyeIntensity`.
 3. Each expression: find its canonical ARKit name with `canonicalArkitName`. When `blendShapes` has a range for that name, `t = clamp((v − inputMin) / (inputMax − inputMin), 0, 1)` and `v' = outputMin + t × (outputMax − outputMin)`. Then `v'' = clamp(v' × blendShapeSensitivity, 0, 1)`. A name with no canonical form gets the sensitivity only. The expression keeps the name that the sample used.
 4. Linked blink: when `linkedBlink` is on and the sample has both blink channels (canonical `eyeBlinkLeft` and `eyeBlinkRight`), both get the larger value.
The stale fade weight applies after the mapping, as today. Expression suppression (B4) applies after the composer, as today.
R11. The tracking source Inspector gets a **Face** group under Smoothing (in `TrackingAdvanced`) for each source kind: Head intensity, Head offset (three numbers), Eye intensity, Sensitivity, Linked blink, and a **Blend shapes** list. The list shows one row for each name in `ARKIT_NAMES` with the four range numbers; a row with no range shows the default values (0, 1, 0, 1) and commits a range only when the user changes it; **Reset** on a row removes its range. Number fields use the kit `Input`. The kit has no checkbox, so Linked blink uses the native checkbox that `TrackingSourceFields` already uses; a kit checkbox would replace it. Each commit sends one `setTrackingMapping`, through the same `model.ts`, `EditorApp.svelte`, and `Inspector.svelte` path as smoothing.

### Finger source (TRK-05)

R12. `AvatarTracking` gets an optional field `fingersSourceId: Id | null`. A missing field means `null`. In Rust, `AvatarTracking` becomes an explicit struct (not the `object!` macro) with `#[serde(default, skip_serializing_if = "Option::is_none")]` on the new field, so a show without it round-trips unchanged. The reference rule (`schema.ts`, the avatar check near the end of the scene refinements, and the Rust rule in `crates/contracts/src/lib.rs`) checks all three slots: each referenced source exists, and an `ifacialmocap` source can be only a face source. `removeEntity` of a source clears it from each of the three slots (`scene-ops.ts`).
R13. In `evaluate`, when an avatar has a finger source with a live input, each finger bone (the 30 bones `leftThumbMetacarpal` to `rightLittleDistal`) that the finger sample has comes from the finger source with its weight and replaces the same bone of the body source. A finger bone that the finger sample does not have (for example `hands` off, or a hand lost for a frame) stays from the body source. When the finger source has no live input, the body source's finger bones apply, as today. Bones other than finger bones from the finger source are not used.
R14. The B5 rule (R12 of the B5 contract) covers the new slot: a `scenePatch` fails when its end state has a source that two avatars use as body, face, or finger source and its start state did not. One avatar can use one source in more than one slot.
R15. The character Inspector (`AvatarSourceFields`) gets a **Fingers source** select after Face source. It lists VMC and MediaPipe sources. `sourceOptions` in `apps/desktop/src/editor/live-fields.ts` gets the channel `'fingers'` (no iFacialMocap), and its set of used sources counts the finger slot of the other character, so each of the three selects disables a source that the other character uses in any slot, as B5 R13 does. `AvatarSourceFields` keeps all three IDs when one select changes.

### MCP and documents

R16. The `scene_patch` tool description names `calibrateTracking`, `setTrackingCalibration`, `setTrackingMapping`, and `fingersSourceId` in `setAvatarTracking`. No tool is added.

## O — Operations

1. Contracts: TypeScript and Rust tests that accept a source with `calibration` and `mapping` and an avatar with `fingersSourceId`; reject 0 or 7 calibration entries, a bone outside the set, a non-unit quaternion, each mapping limit (intensity 2.01, offset 91, sensitivity 3.01, `inputMin = inputMax`, `outputMin > outputMax`, a non-ARKit key), and an iFacialMocap finger source; accept an old show without the fields. A JSON fixture with the fields and one without them round-trip through both languages unchanged (no `null` is added).
2. Calibration (unit): capture takes only the six bones with their spaces; capture of a sample with no such bone returns null; apply makes the captured rotation identity; apply skips a bone with another space; apply leaves other bones unchanged; a head turned 30° after calibration at 10° reads 20°; with a normalized chest and head (the MediaPipe shape), calibrated at chest pitch 10° and face yaw 5°, a sample with chest pitch 25° and face yaw 35° gives `chest' × head'` equal to `inverse(chestN × headN) × (chest × head)` within 1e-6, and the composed world face turns 30° from neutral when the chest does not move.
3. Mapping (unit): intensity 0, 0.5, 1, and 2 on a 40° head turn (0°, 20°, 40°, 80°); intensity 2 on a 130° turn gives a rotation of 260° about the same axis; offset `[0, 15, 0]` on identity gives the quaternion `[0, sin 7.5°, 0, cos 7.5°]` (it turns +Z toward +X); offset `[10, 15, 0]` equals `qY(15°) × qX(10°)`; eye intensity; a range (input 0.2..0.8, output 0..1: 0.5 → 0.5, 0.1 → 0, 0.9 → 1); output max 0 disables; sensitivity 2 clamps at 1; `eyeBlink_L` and `eyeBlinkLeft` both find the `eyeBlinkLeft` range and keep their own name; linked blink gives both the larger value; the default mapping is identity on a sample.
4. Composer (unit): order (calibration before mapping before fade); a stale source fades the mapped value; the MediaPipe head and chest composition still holds with a calibrated chest; the finger source replaces finger bones only; a finger sample with only the left-hand fingers keeps the body source's right-hand fingers; a finger source with no live input falls back to the body source; two avatars with their own finger sources stay independent.
5. Session (unit): `calibrateTracking` on a receiving source writes the calibration and one revision; it fails before the first `advance`, on a disabled source, a stale sample (601 ms old at the last `advance` clock), a sample of an old generation, a sample without calibration bones, and in a patch that also has `setTrackingConfig` or `removeEntity` for that source; a second Calibrate replaces the calibration; `setTrackingConfig` keeps it, except a MediaPipe `mirror` change, which removes it; `removeEntity` of a finger source clears the slot and the scene stays valid; a patch that gives the finger slot of one avatar to the body source of the other fails; one source in the body and finger slots of one avatar passes.
6. MediaPipe (unit): the rewritten retargeter test sends a face matrix turned 30° as the first frame and reads 30° (it read 0° before, so the test fails on the old code); the face along the camera axis reads identity; the expression clamp checks of the old test stay.
7. Editor: unit tests for the mapping draft parse, for `sourceOptions` with the `'fingers'` channel (a source in the other character's finger slot is disabled in its body select too), and for `AvatarSourceFields` keeping the finger ID when the body source changes. Headless Chromium check of the editor flow with a mock source: Calibrate is disabled while the source waits; mock samples sent through the browser client's `tracking.submit` (the path that `App.svelte` uses for MediaPipe samples) make it enabled; if the headless harness has no session clock for that path, the run says so in the evidence file and covers the button state with a component test; Calibrate then Reset change the scene revision; a Face group change commits one `setTrackingMapping`; the Fingers source select disables the other character's source.
8. MCP: a test that `scene_patch` with `calibrateTracking` on a source with no sample returns the R3 error, and the descriptions name the operations.
9. Documents: the evidence file with the commit, platform, backend, each check and its raw result, and the gaps; parity notes (R17); `ARCHITECTURE.md` (R17).

## N — Norms

- Unit tests for the schemas, calibration, mapping, composer order, and session rules. No new integration test: this slice adds no process, network, file, or protocol boundary.
- UI copy is the control label only (constitution principle 1). Calibrate has no instruction sentence; the Warudo step ("look straight ahead") stays in the handbook-style docs, not in the Inspector. Controls come from `@oshi-connect/ui`.
- Functional TypeScript and plain data. Calibration and mapping are pure functions on a sample.
- Mock samples prove the math. They do not prove the look with a real phone or camera.

## S — Safeguards

- Do not change `TrackingSample`, `TrackingStatus`, `AvatarPose`, the source limit (4), or the avatar limit (2).
- Do not make an old show fail to load. The new fields are optional.
- Do not keep a calibration or a neutral outside the scene state.
- Do not add a dependency, a CI job, or a vendor service.
- Do not claim a macOS, OBS, phone, or camera result. These stay for the owner: Calibrate with an iPhone (iFacialMocap) on macOS makes a straight-ahead head neutral, and it stays after a restart of the app with the saved show; Calibrate with a physical webcam (MediaPipe) does the same for the head and the upper body; head intensity, head offset, a blend shape range, sensitivity, and linked blink look correct on a perfect sync VRM in the preview and in OBS with alpha; MediaPipe fingers with a VMC body on one character; continued motion while the editor is minimized.
